A. Channel cards

Most scannable

One card per channel in a two-column grid. Each card is a self-contained panel with its own status, details and actions. States are shown side by side, so “which channels work” reads in one glance.

Trade-off: great for 4 to 6 channels, gets long with many accounts per channel (a Page list inside a card). Details stay shallow, no history.

B. Channel rail + detail pane

Best for depth

A left rail lists channels with a status dot; the right pane is one channel at a time, with its accounts, webhook subscription, health and recent activity. Each channel really is “its own UI”, and adding Telegram later is one more rail item.

Trade-off: you cannot see all channels’ detail at once, only status dots. On a phone it becomes list, then detail (two screens). More work to build than A.

C. Health first, grouped rows

Operator view

A one-line health summary on top, then rows grouped by what you can do: Connected, Available, Coming soon. A row expands in place to its panel. Anything that needs attention floats to the top with the fix next to it.

Trade-off: the densest and quickest for someone who checks channels daily and wants “is anything broken?” answered first. Less inviting for a first-time user than the cards in A.
